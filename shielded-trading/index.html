The Billion DollarDrain:

MEV, Censorship, and the Illusion of Fair Trading

Since 2020, over $1.8 billion has been lost to MEV on Ethereum, largely at the expense of everyday users. This isn’t just a byproduct of market dynamics; it’s the result of a flawed architecture where all pending transactions are broadcast publicly. At this stage, specialized bots and validators exploit and censor transactions.
Shutter's encrypted mempool system addresses this issue by temporarily hiding all transactions in the public mempool (the waiting area for transactions to be confirmed). This stops manipulation at the source.

How Encrypted Mempools Protect You Using Threshold Encryption

Transaction Encryption
Your transaction is encrypted with a DKG-generated key via a decentralized group of Keypers before it enters the public mempool, which is a waiting area for transactions.
Preventing Manipulation
Attackers can’t see your encrypted transaction, making it nearly impossible for them to front-run or disrupt your trades.
Decentralized Decryption
Once the block producer or sequencer confirms inclusion and ordering for your transaction, the decentralized group of Keypers generate the decryption key for your transaction, which is then decrypted and processed as normal.

A Distributed Solution to a Centralized Problem

Shutter's encrypted mempools employ decentralized threshold encryption — a form of MPC (Multi-Party Computation). Multiple parties known as Keypers (which are governed by the Shutter DAO) are required to decrypt transactions, making collusion more difficult. It's the ideal solution for both protecting users and preventing the centralized concentration of power.
